Compressor design app - guide

The app designs an axial compressor at the mean line, shows every possible design point on interactive maps, lets you pick the point you want, designs it row by row and sends it to the off-design app for the full compressor maps.

Configurations

Rotor-stator stages; rotor-rotor on one shaft; rotor-rotor on two shafts (same direction or counter-rotating).

Any size

1 to 12 stages or rotors. Stages repeat stage 1; any stage can get its own φ, ψ, R or DF.

Choose visually

Four linked maps of the whole design space; click a point or let criteria find the best one.

Off-design link

One button saves the design file and loads it into the off-design app.

Step 2 Compute the design space

Press Step 2 - Compute the design space ▶. Every combination of the flow coefficient φ, the loading coefficient ψ and the reaction R (and of U2/U1 on two shafts) is designed through all the rows. A progress bar shows the advance; 65 000 points take about a second, a two-shaft space with a million points some seconds.

A point is feasible when it is physical and respects every limit of the right panel (solidity, inlet Mach number, de Haller ratio, hub/tip ratio, h/c, exit swirl). Changing a limit updates the feasible region at once.

The best point for the chosen objective is selected automatically after the computation (black square on the maps). Nothing is designed in detail until you press Use this point ▶ Design tab.

Step 3 Choose the design point (Design space tab)

  1. Map controls. Which slice the maps show: the reaction R (design and contour maps), the speed ratio (two shafts), the solidity of which row (design map), the φ of the sizing lines, how many lines, which quantity colours the PR-η map and which quantity the contour map draws.
  2. Design map η against the solidity, lines of constant φ (blue) and ψ (orange), dashed lines at the solidity limits.
  3. Sizing map pressure ratio against the inlet area per unit mass flow: grey = every feasible point, lines of constant R (green) and ψ (violet) at the chosen φ.
  4. PR-η map of all feasible points, coloured by the quantity you choose, with the Pareto front (black): no point is both more efficient and of higher pressure ratio than a point on the front.
  5. Contour map of any quantity over φ and ψ at the chosen R; the black line bounds the feasible region.
  6. Objective and constraints. What "best" means and which points may be chosen. Changes update the candidates at once.
  7. Find the best point and the ten best candidates; click a row to select that candidate.
  8. Selected point shows its values and status. Use this point ▶ Design tab designs it in detail and opens the Design tab.

Three ways to select a point

By criteria
Choose the objective, tick constraints (e.g. η ≥ 0.90), press Find the best point. The best point and nine runners-up are listed.
By clicking a map
Click near any point of any of the four maps: the nearest point shown is selected and all maps move to its slice. Use the axes toolbar to zoom first if points are dense.
By typing (Design tab): enter φ, ψ, R (and U2/U1) and press Design the typed point - any value, also between the grid values.

Reading the maps

Colour of the PR-η map
The two axes are always pressure ratio and efficiency. The colour adds a third quantity: with "Flow coefficient φ" you see which φ gives the points along the Pareto front, with "A₁/ṁ" which points give a small machine, with "Highest inlet Mach" which points approach the Mach limit.
Contour map shows
Choose any quantity (efficiency, pressure ratio, area, highest solidity, Mach number, de Haller ratio, hub/tip ratio, blade speed, mean radius, exit swirl) to see how it varies over φ and ψ - for instance where the hub/tip ratio becomes too small. Infeasible regions are blank (or shown with "Show infeasible points").
Hover the labels of the map controls for a short explanation. The title of each map says what it shows, at which R and speed ratio, and which quantity the colours represent.

Step 4 Design tab

  1. Design point of stage 1 (rotor 1). It is filled by Use this point; you can also type any point and press Design the typed point. Send to the off-design app ▶ is available here too.
  2. Stage table: one line per stage (rotor-stator) or rotor (rotor-rotor). See Editing stages.
  3. Summary: pressure ratio, efficiency, temperature ratio, work, power, speeds, mean radius, inlet area, hub/tip ratios, blades.
  4. Blade rows: one line per row with every aerodynamic and blading quantity.
  5. Stations: inlet and the exit of every row.
  6. Status and warnings: which limits the design violates and any doubtful value, in plain words.

Editing stages

Every cell shows the value that stage uses. To give one stage its own value, double-click the cell, type the value and press Enter. The design is recomputed at once and the cell turns yellow. Row 1 (blue) is the design point itself: editing it changes the design point.

Geometry tab

The third plot shows the blade sections at the mean radius: NACA-65 metal angles on a circular-arc camber line, with an illustrative 10 % thickness, three blades per row at the real pitch (chord / solidity).

Compare designs tab

Every design you keep (Add current design in the Design, Export or Compare tab) is drawn here over the others, one colour per design. You can also add design files written by this app (they are rebuilt from their design record) or project files (their whole design list).

  1. Pressure ratio vs efficiency: filled marker = total-to-total efficiency, open marker = efficiency if the exit swirl is not recovered.
  2. Size: pressure ratio vs inlet area per unit mass flow.
  3. Annulus: tip and hub lines and the mean radius of every design.
  4. Per row: solidity, de Haller ratio, Mach number, loss, DF, angles, incidence, deviation, row pressure ratio, blades, h/c (circles rotors, squares stators).
  5. Through the machine: pressure ratio, efficiency, temperature ratio, swirl angle, axial velocity, static state, hub/tip ratio, blade height, area at every station.
  6. Velocity triangles of the chosen row for all designs (C thick, W thin; inlet solid, exit dashed).

The plots save as PNG (300 dpi) and the table as Excel or CSV. Per-stage values typed in the Design tab are kept in project files but not in design files; a rebuilt design that differs from its file is reported.

Step 5 Export & off-design

  • Send to the off-design app ▶ saves the design file on your computer (it asks where the first time), opens the off-design app if it is not open, selects the right compressor type and loads the file. Press Run there.
  • Variables written to the design file: every variable is listed with its meaning, size and whether the off-design app requires it. Untick what you do not want; the presets set common choices. The app refuses to save silently without a required variable or without stator blading where it is needed.
  • Excel: sheets Summary, Rows, Stations, Inputs, Off-design variables, Candidates, Design list and, if ticked, the whole feasible design space.
  • PNG: the four maps and the three geometry plots at 300 dpi, named after the case.
  • Design list: keep several designs (any configuration) side by side, reload one, or save them all as design files at once.
  • Projects (left panel): save and reopen the inputs, the point, the stage values, the design list and, optionally, the design space.

Method

Stage 1 follows from φ = Cx/U, ψ = Δh0/U2 and R:

tan α1 = (1 − R − ψ/2)/φ,   tan β1 = (R + ψ/2)/φ,   tan α2 = (1 − R + ψ/2)/φ,   tan β2 = (R − ψ/2)/φ

The rotor-1 relative Mach number Mr1 gives C1, then U = Cx/φ and rm = 60U/(2πN). Every row is marched at constant mean radius:

  • solidity from the diffusion factor (Lieblein): σ = ΔWθ / [2W1(DF − 1 + W2/W1)]
  • profile loss: ω = (θ/c)(DF) · 2σ / cos β2, with the selected correlation for θ/c(DF)
  • metal angles from the NACA-65 incidence charts and Carter's deviation rule (charts valid for 0.5 ≤ σ ≤ 2)
  • areas from continuity A = ṁRT/(pCx) at every station, hub/tip ratio from the area and the mean radius
  • rotor-stator: the stator of stage k turns the flow to the inlet angle of stage k+1; rotor-rotor: every rotor adds ψU of swirl in its own frame; on two shafts the even rotors turn at (U2/U1)·U, with the opposite sign when counter-rotating

Troubleshooting

ProblemWhat to do
Buttons do nothing, or errors such as "Unable to find function" after replacing the app filesClose every app window, type clear classes in the Command Window and start the app again.
"No feasible point"Relax a limit (e.g. the inlet Mach number), or widen the ranges. Tick "Show infeasible points" to see where the points are; the Selected point box gives the reason of any point.
The maps do not match the Design tabThe status bar says when the inputs changed after the computation: press Step 2 again.
Solidity outside 0.5-2The NACA-65/Carter charts are extrapolated; change DF or the point, or accept it knowingly (warning in the Design tab).
"Send to the off-design app" says the app cannot receive designsUse the off-design app of step 27 or later. The design file is saved anyway: load it there with "mat file input".
